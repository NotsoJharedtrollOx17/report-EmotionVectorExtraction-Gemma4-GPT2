\begin{abstract}

We report a first-pass, resource-constrained partial replication of selected analyses from Anthropic's study of emotion 
concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5. Using generated emotion-story corpora, we extract affect-associated residual-stream 
directions from \texttt{openai-community/gpt2-medium} and \texttt{google/gemma-4-E2B} and examine them with Logit
Lens projections, principal component analysis (PCA), cosine similarity, and activation steering. Across nine- and 
20-emotion sets, both models exhibit several label-consistent vocabulary projections and local cosine-similarity families. 
Their PCA plots show a partial valence-like separation along PC1, but PC2 does not consistently support an arousal interpretation. 
Next-token probability comparisons span eleven coefficients from $-0.5$ to $+0.5$; displayed heatmaps and inspected sampled
continuations use $+0.5$. Effects depend on the prompt and direction. Both steered and unsteered samples contain repetition,
markup, or unrelated content, so these samples do not isolate steering's contribution to generation problems. These observations
qualitatively converge with prior Claude and Gemma 4 E4B reports on some geometric patterns, while revealing model- and 
label-set-dependent structure in two additional models. The corpus is generated and uncurated, the evaluation reuses 
vector-derived target tokens, and no external affect 
ratings or control directions are included. Accordingly, these results describe this extraction-and-intervention pipeline; 
they do not establish universal or uniquely emotional representations.

\end{abstract}
